# Chess Masti AI — notes for future Claude

Orientation for anyone (human or agent) picking up this codebase cold. Terse and load-bearing on purpose. For the fuller audit picture, see [AUDIT_NOTES.md](AUDIT_NOTES.md).

## What this is

Next.js 15 app (`chess-coach-ai/`, deploys to Vercel as **chessmasti.com**). Two tightly coupled surfaces: the AI coaching model and the web UI that wraps it. Chess rules via `chess.js`, board via `react-chessboard`, analysis via `stockfish.js` (WASM, in-browser Web Worker). Mixed Pages Router (`src/pages/*.tsx`) + App Router (`src/app/api/*` for route handlers, `src/app/layout.tsx`).

## AI architecture (the one mental model that matters)

Every server-side LLM call funnels through [`callLLM()`](src/lib/llmProvider.ts) in [src/lib/llmProvider.ts:216](src/lib/llmProvider.ts#L216). Anthropic Claude (Sonnet 4 flagship / Haiku 4.5 fast) is primary, OpenAI (gpt-4o / gpt-4o-mini) is the fallback. Any 4xx/5xx/network error on Anthropic triggers an immediate retry on OpenAI so a single user request is never dropped.

Callers use a `tier` (`"flagship" | "fast"`) — never a model name. Keep it that way.

Callsites (as of 2026-05-30):

- [src/app/api/chat/route.ts](src/app/api/chat/route.ts) — follow-up chat (`fast` → Haiku 4.5). Uses a server-cached context keyed by `contextId` from the prior analysis call. Since 2026-09-25 it has its own prompt, [src/lib/prompts/followUpPrompt.ts](src/lib/prompts/followUpPrompt.ts) (the idea behind the move and what actually happens, the line drawn by the app, a "Lesson:" with the pattern's name and the check to run next time, an optional "Your turn:" question; a 100-word budget, 140 for a walkthrough; `[CONTINUATION:n:c]` / `[PLAYED:n:c]` line tokens; `COACH_FOLLOWUP_PROMPT=legacy` restores the turn-1 prompt), grounds on the move the question names ([src/lib/coach/questionAnchor.ts](src/lib/coach/questionAnchor.ts), returned to the client as `anchor` so the board moves there) and sends a context cut to that move ([src/lib/coach/followUpContext.ts](src/lib/coach/followUpContext.ts)) instead of the whole `buildCompactGameContext()` summary. Output cap 600 tokens, history trimmed to four exchanges. The follow-up referee ([src/lib/contract/followUpReferee.ts](src/lib/contract/followUpReferee.ts)) licenses a numbered move only at its own move number (the game's move there, a licensed line's move there, or a legal alternative there) and reads a sentence's moves as a line: on 2026-09-26 a live answer borrowed "Kxc7" from the next key moment's line and the number-blind pool let it through three times. Moves are licensed as written, case kept (the game's bxc4 never licenses Bxc4), and a figure that ends a sentence is read ("at -2.84."), both since 2026-10-09. With an anchor the contract block is focused on that move (`ContractFocus` in [src/lib/contract/followUp.ts](src/lib/contract/followUp.ts)): its engine line and story alone, the other findings' verdicts and evals with their lines withheld. When the Mastermind validators reject the model's draft, the follow-up path serves the draft minus the sentences they contradicted (`lastDraft` on the regenerate result, `flaggedSpans` on the referee, `pipeline.servedDraft` in the response) rather than the pipeline's "What changed / What to look at" template, which answers no question; a timeout, the legacy prompt and a context without a contract still get the template. Since 2026-10-08 a follow-up may carry `clientEvals`, the client's what-if search ([src/lib/coach/clientEvals.ts](src/lib/coach/clientEvals.ts), the wire type and its verifier, client-safe): under `COACH_WHATIF_EVALS=1` the route verifies it against the stored game (shape and bounds; the position replayed from the start and the client's FEN equal byte for byte; every move and line move legal in UCI as chess.js writes it, so a stray promotion suffix is not a second spelling of a move; the played move the game's; the best the review's, and left out when the review has no real line there; a mate scored for the mating side and at the distance the line shows; every move from the search at the payload's depth), moves the anchor to its ply with the alternative asked about (`anchorAtIndex`, comparing the server's own SANs as written, so Bxc4 is not bxc4; the words' reading is logged beside it as `wordsPly`), tells its moves and numbers in the anchor block as a search of their own (`WHAT-IF SEARCH`, each figure followed by its perspective, never "the engine rates"; the game's own move asked about by name is "the move played, asked about"), and gives the eval validator a reference per move, with the review's own number beside the cold one where the review has one (`positionEvals` on the pipeline; the played move's eval after it, the review's best its eval before). The validator checks a claim whose spans name exactly one what-if move (a numbered mention only at its own number and side, a bare one only when the game never played that SAN elsewhere, a pawn push only numbered or cued and never a square, O-O never inside O-O-O, 0-0 read as O-O) against that move's numbers alone, the figure and the band against the same number, so a correct number about the alternative is no longer flagged against the played move's eval; a claim that names none, or several, is checked as before (which move a figure belongs to is not guessed). A payload that fails is dropped with its reason and the turn is answered as without it, never a 400 (`clientEvals` is `z.unknown()` on the schema); the outcome is echoed as `gameAnalysis.clientEvals`. A what-if at the final position or in a game set up from a position is dropped (the route replays from the standard start). Without a what-if the referee is byte for byte what it was; with one, a sentence it drops is read once more with the what-if's roots (`replacing` on `LicensedLine`): an unnumbered alternative may open a sentence on its own line ("Nd6+ exd6"), after nothing but the move it replaces ("Instead of Nc7+, Nd6+ exd6"), matched as written (Bxc4 is not bxc4), and every move after it must be that line's next move ("Nd6+ exd6 Nxa8" does not borrow the game's 9. Nxa8). The lines stay out of the shared ply table and the pool, so no other line borrows their moves ("8. Qxc1 exd6" is dropped), the game's own move is no root, the what-if's numbers are licensed only in a sentence that names their move (`licensedEvalsByMove`: +2.51 for 8. Qxc1, never for 8. Nc7+), and a sentence kept without the what-if is kept with it. A numbered move borrowed from another licensed line at its own number ("8. Qxc1 Rb8 9. Nxa8") still passes, as it did before. **The board answers a what-if before the coach does.** Since 2026-10-07 a question that names an alternative ("what about Nd6+ instead?", "why not 8. Qxc1?") is read on the client with the chat route's own resolvers ([src/components/preview-analysis/coachWhatIf.ts](src/components/preview-analysis/coachWhatIf.ts) over `questionAnchor.ts` and `questionIntent.ts`), the asked move is turned into a move legal in the position it is asked about, and it is scored beside the game's move and the review's best there in one `evaluateMoves` search (one `searchmoves` search, one depth, a cold table, White-relative). Where the alternative is played from (`resolveWhatIf`): it is drawn only when the question and the game agree on one reading, and a side the words give the move ("could I have played", "what if Black plays", "why didn't my opponent play", "my Nd5", "for White"; "I" and "my opponent" only once the player's side is known) must be the side that plays it; otherwise nothing is drawn and the coach answers in words. After an "after" line (one move or several, "after 7... Qxc1 8. Nc7+", "after my opponent's Bc4"), found where the game played it, the alternative is the next move, or with "instead" or a number placing it there may replace the line's last move; a line the game never played draws nothing. A numbered move sits at its own number counted from the game's root, and beside another named move the one the game played at that number is set aside and the other is the alternative. "Move 8" with no side reads both sides' move 8 and, legal for both, takes the confirmed side. Two moves named: one where the game played it and the other in its place, read the words' way round ("why X instead of Y?" asks about Y, any other form about X: "what about Qxc1 instead of Nc7+?" and "why Nc7+ instead of Qxc1?" are both 8. Qxc1). A move is the game's by what it does, not its spelling ("Nd4" finds the game's Nxd4), and case tells a piece from a pawn (Bxc4 is not bxc4). A bare alternative with "instead" replaces the move the strip names (the ply before the cursor), with "here" or "now" beside it (after a short predicate too, "is Nd5 better here?", or opening the clause that asks, "Here, what about Nd5?") is the next move, and legal at both with neither cue, or with both and no side named, draws nothing. More than two moves besides an "after" line draw nothing, and a bare pawn push is a move only beside a cue ("why not d4?", "d4 instead") or inside an "after" line. The player's message carries the ask (`whatIf` on `CoachMessage`) and the state lives in a store outside React (`WhatIfStore`, one per page; `WhatIfLine` reads its own id with `useSyncExternalStore`, so a partial re-renders the line and not the page, which cost half a second per partial on the throttled phone), from "checking" at the moment the message is pushed, so the block under it (`WhatIfLine`: two reserved lines of numbers, the compared moves without a repeated move number, then the asked move and the engine's reply as a `ProofLine`, or `ProofLinePlaceholder` until then) is at its full height before the engine answers and nothing moves when it does. The line paints its moves first and their captions a frame later (`deferCaptions` on `ProofLine`, the caption row empty until then, never "a quiet move" by default): working out eight plies of facts took about 0.4 s of the throttled phone's two-second budget before the line could paint. Once the reader taps or plays the line its moves are pinned for good (`data-pinned`), so a deeper search never swaps them under the reader, Play again included; only a final search with no line for the asked move takes the line away. On the first partial at depth 10 or deeper the asked move goes on the board through the exploration preview (`launchWhatIf` builds it with `buildLinePreview` and sets the cursor itself, without `revealBoard`: on a phone the line under the question is the answer in hand), unless the reader moved on, opened an exploration, switched view, started a drill or a later reply jumped meanwhile; the line deepens to 16 under the reader, and the search is given up after 30 s whether or not the engine answers, so it can never hold the engine. The coach's request runs beside it: the question from the send box waits for the what-if's first partial at depth 10 or deeper, at most 1.5 s (`whatIfEvalsWithin`), and goes up with its numbers as `clientEvals` (`whatIfClientEvals`: every scored move's own number, depth and line cut to eight plies, verified by the route; none for a game set up from a position or a what-if past the last move), marked `coach-what-if:sent`. A bare follow-up asked on the what-if's own alternative is the next alternative at the same ply, unless it is also the reply on the board shown and nothing in the words (a replace cue, a side) picks the replacement, and a reading elsewhere in the game of a move that is also that reply draws nothing. The engine is handed round in turns ([src/lib/engine/engineTurn.ts](src/lib/engine/engineTurn.ts), `engineTurn`, one per engine instance): the live-eval effect and a what-if queue behind each other instead of each calling `stopAllCurrentJobs` on the other, because the engine's own queue drops a superseded search's promise; a what-if is asked `ahead` of the live evals still waiting and aborts the live search in progress (`evaluatePositionWithUpdate` and `evaluateMoves` take an `AbortSignal` since 2026-10-07, raced against every step before the search and the cloud head start; the live-eval effect aborts its own search in its cleanup, and a newer what-if, a new game, a review restart ("busy") or the page going aborts a what-if still searching; a live eval the what-if stopped is asked again when the board did not move), so the first partial is not behind a depth-18 search. A shut-down engine refuses commands, stops its sweep and starts no worker, with an `EngineShutDownError` the page's sweep ignores (an engine swap shuts the old engine down before the sweep's effect is cleaned up); MultiPV is recorded when the option is posted, so a search aborted in that phase cannot leave the record behind the worker. The coach's jump for the what-if's own reply (`whatIfReplySink`, with the hold in `createWhatIfJumpGate`) is skipped once the line is drawn, held while it is checking and applied if no line comes; every other reply, including a later question about the same move, jumps as before through `coachSink`. The three eval regimes (the review's warm sweep, the live effect's deeper number, the what-if's cold pair) are still never subtracted across each other. [tests/e2e/local/coach-what-if.spec.ts](tests/e2e/local/coach-what-if.spec.ts) holds the pathway's two-second budget on the page's own clock (`performance.mark`, `coach-what-if:asked|partial|drawn|board`, plus `sent`, `preempted` when a live search still running was stopped, which then marks `coach-live-eval:aborted`, `jump-held|jump-skipped`, and `coach-live-eval:search`; the words-first order the jump's hold is for, and a live search still running at the ask, are made certain by holding the engine worker's messages from an init script, `holdableEngine`, not raced): on the throttled phone leg the line is drawn about 1.2 s after the ask, and the harness's own wait for it is reported beside that figure, not asserted, because the exploration preview's render after the draw keeps the main thread for a second or two there.

**The page does what it is told, without the coach.** Since 2026-10-08, behind `NEXT_PUBLIC_COACH_PAGE_ACTIONS` (off until its flip: `PAGE_ACTIONS_DEFAULT` in [src/lib/coach/pageActions.ts](src/lib/coach/pageActions.ts) is the one line the flip changes, the env overrides it either way, and the CI e2e legs build with it on), an order typed to the coach ("flip the board", "go to move 20", "next move", "back", "go to the start", "play the line again", "I was Black", "back to my side") is read in `handleSend` before any fetch, anchor or what-if and carried out on the page. Words that are orders as often as questions ("next", "start over", "move 20", "last move") are not orders, and neither is "keep it short" or "be more detailed" (depth is never a setting). **A follow-up can be about the other side.** Since 2026-10-08, behind `COACH_PERSPECTIVE=1` (server, read per call, off until its flip, and only on the follow-up prompt: under `COACH_FOLLOWUP_PROMPT=legacy` nothing is read), a turn has a subject: the side whose decisions the answer is about, never the addressee. The player stays "you" whichever side it is, so the player's colour is never replaced by it (the anchor block's "the player's move", the referee's "your", the validators' perspective, `compact.playerColor`). [src/lib/coach/questionPerspective.ts](src/lib/coach/questionPerspective.ts) reads it from the question's words ("from Black's side", "what was my opponent thinking", "how should Black have played", "Black's worst move", "where did he go wrong", "what's Black's best move"), else from `perspective` on the request (`z.unknown()`, "w"/"b"/"white"/"black"; the client sends it from 2.6, and a field equal to the player's side is no subject and ends one the history carried), else from the latest kept history question about the game played (a view, what the side was thinking, what it did or should have done, its mistakes; never a present-tense or board question). A question in the first person ("I", "we", "my" but not "my opponent"), naming the player's colour, saying "here" or "now", or naming the player's side ends the carry, and a carry a later kept question ended stays ended; "tell me more" and "I don't understand" continue it. A colour always resolves; "my opponent", "he", "my side" only when the stored tail confirms the player's side (`confirmedSideOf`: the "- The user is playing as:" line names the context's own colour and no "is NOT confirmed" line stands beside it). Two sides named is no subject. "I was Black", "coach me as Black", "as White resigned", "switch sides", "from Black's side of the board", "they" beside "my rooks", a mistake or best move named in a first-person question ("did I punish Black's blunder?") and a side beside a move number are not subjects. Under the flag the anchor reads owners whatever the subject: "Black's move 20", "my 12th move", "my opponents 12th move", "move 20 for Black" or "by my opponent" (but "the eval after move 7 for Black" is in Black's favour), a side as the verb's subject ("what did Black play on move 12"), and "after Black's move 7" as the reply only when the question asks for a move ("what should I have played") and as that move itself when it asks about the position. A relative owner with the side unconfirmed names no move. "20. Ka7" is Black's 20th when Ka7 is Black's move there and no move for White. A bare "move N" and a bare move played by both sides follow a subject from the words or the field (never the other side's move in its place when the words named it, and a move the side never played then gets one fact line, "Black played no move 44. The game ended after 44. Qa7.", in a game that replays from the start only; 2.6 must move the page's own "go to move N" with the field); with no subject, or one the history carried, they are read exactly as without the flag. ## Auth model (post-school-WiFi migration, 2026-04-28)

**Auth and Firestore reads no longer hit Firebase from the browser.** This was forced by school-network filters that block `*.firebaseapp.com` and `firestore.googleapis.com`. Everything routes through chessmasti.com server-side. Mental model:

- **Sessions**: signed JWT in an httpOnly cookie (`cm_session`). Sign-key in `SESSION_SECRET`. See [src/lib/auth/session.ts](src/lib/auth/session.ts).
- **Email/password auth**: bcrypt-hashed `passwordHash` field on the user doc. Endpoints under [src/app/api/auth/](src/app/api/auth/) — `signup`, `signin`, `signout`, `me`, `password`, `forgot-password`, `reset-password`.
- **Google OAuth**: server-routed via [src/app/api/auth/google/start](src/app/api/auth/google/start) + `callback`. The redirect_uri is chessmasti.com — never `*.firebaseapp.com`. Account-links by email so the existing 50 Firebase Auth users keep their UID and saved games when they sign in via Google for the first time.
- **Firestore reads**: server-side via Firebase Admin SDK ([src/lib/server/firebaseAdmin.ts](src/lib/server/firebaseAdmin.ts)). Browser calls `/api/users/me`, `/api/games`, etc. — never `firestore.googleapis.com` directly.

[/api/enhanced-analysis](src/app/api/enhanced-analysis/route.ts) reads the session cookie and threads prefs into `getCoachChatSystemPrompt`.
- **Intern mode (CMIP)**: at sign-in (Google OAuth + email/password), the auth callbacks check the email against the Supabase `intern_allowlist` table via [src/lib/intern/allowlist.ts](src/lib/intern/allowlist.ts) and stamp `isIntern: boolean` into the `cm_session` JWT claim. Flipping the allowlist takes effect on next sign-in. The browser reads `isIntern` from `/api/auth/me` via [useAuth](src/contexts/AuthContext.tsx) / [useViewer](src/hooks/useViewer.ts); intern-only UI (header EmployeePill, InternalNavLinks, InternalHomeCard, `/intern` dashboard, blue theme swap) is gated on this flag. It fails on a changed or broken corpus, a network attempt, a numbered move in served text that is legal on no board the game, its stored lines or a verified what-if reach at that ply, or that is numbered past the game's end (unless HEAD's referee now drops its sentence, or the manifest acknowledges it with a reason), a question the page writes routed by the model, a turn making more than one routing call, a drift in the story probe's pins, and, for a server flag listed in `REPLAY_MANIFEST.flipped`, a newest run per validator wing at HEAD's prompt versions that misses the bar (lean: route-clock p50 under 5000 ms and median words at rest of 60 or fewer over at least 20 turns, fielded: no invented move, eval figure or undrawable proof on a fielded turn, router: the six no-board and acknowledgement rows of intent-60 present). Everything else it measures is printed, never failed, each clock on its own (the tester CSV is the client's HTTP clock, a probe's `timing` the route's, `harnessMs` the probe's). A server flag's flip PR adds the flag to `flipped` beside a committed run, and a re-pin of a frozen file or a story-probe count is a deliberate edit to the manifest. The move oracle ([scripts/eval/replay/oracle.ts](scripts/eval/replay/oracle.ts)) reads numbered moves as the referee does (`numberedPly`, Black only with three dots, and the sentence splitter ends a sentence at two), so a bare move stays the referee's.

Coverage is uneven on purpose: Mastermind validators, the category classifier, `getCoachChatSystemPrompt` (snapshotted per personality), and `llmProvider` fallback have deep tests; insight parsing, Firestore CRUD, the opening-explorer 3-tier fallback, the Maia integration, and SSE stream consumption have **none**. If you're touching one of the uncovered areas, write the test alongside the change — `chessmasti.com` is now small enough that "the next PR" is also the regression you'll have to debug.

The 30+ `test-*.js` files at the repo root are pre-vitest ad-hoc scripts. They aren't wired to `npm test` and don't run in CI. Treat them as dead code unless someone explicitly references one.

**Layout stability (CLS) is measured, not eyeballed.** [tests/e2e/local/layout-stability.spec.ts](tests/e2e/local/layout-stability.spec.ts) holds a per-route CLS budget on the five surfaces that had a real shift, and it only works because it throttles the browser first (`throttleLikeAPhone` in [tests/e2e/helpers.ts](tests/e2e/helpers.ts); it runs in the `local-mobile-light` leg only) — unthrottled, a `next start` paints the finished page before anything can be seen to move and every one of these bugs reports 0.0000. For attribution rather than a pass/fail, [scripts/perf/cls-probe.mjs](scripts/perf/cls-probe.mjs) drives a real Chromium at any base URL (production included) and prints which DOM node moved, when, and by how much.

The family of bug it guards against: **render something, find out what you should have rendered, then render that instead at a different size.** A `dynamic(ssr:false)` with no `loading` (the board is zero pixels tall until its chunk lands), a card gated on state that an effect always fills in, a placeholder whose height or width disagrees with what replaces it, a view rendered before the auth check answers. Reserve the final box from the first frame, and give each branch of a swap its own `key` so React mounts fresh instead of patching nodes in place — a reused node that lands somewhere else IS the layout shift.

One known, deliberate hole: [Lc0DownloadBanner](src/components/Lc0DownloadBanner.tsx) drops ~220px into the top of every non-self-chromed route when `/api/maia-status` reports Maia unhealthy, which is 0.26 of CLS. Production never pays it because Maia is live; CI and a bare checkout have no `MAIA_API_URL` and always do, which is why the specs call `stubMaiaHealthy`. If that banner is ever meant to be seen in anger it has to come in out of flow.
