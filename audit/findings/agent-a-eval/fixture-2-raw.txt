## Core Principles with Bc4

### 1. **Control the Center (d4 and e5)**
- Your bishop on c4 isn't attacking the center directly, but it supports control of **d5** and **e5**
- Don't neglect moves like **d4** when the moment is right—this is often the key breakthrough in the Italian

### 2. **King Safety Before Aggression**
- Bc4 can tempt you into premature attacks (like Ng5 or Bxf7)
- **Castle kingside early** (usually by move 8-10) unless you have a concrete tactical reason not to
- At 1200, protecting your king is more important than launching risky attacks

### 3. **Develop Your Pieces Efficiently**
- Typical move order: **Nf3, d4, O-O, c3, Re1, Bg5 or Bb3**
- Each piece should have a purpose—don't move the same piece twice without a reason

### 4. **Watch f7 (Black's Weakness)**
- f7 is Black's most vulnerable square—it's only defended by the king
- Your Bc4 targets it, but don't attack it without support
- A real threat only exists when you have multiple pieces attacking (like Ng5 + queen support)

## Practical Checklist for Your Game

Before making an aggressive move, ask yourself:
- ✓ Have I castled?
- ✓ Are my pieces developed (Nf3, Nc3)?
- ✓ Is my center secure (d4 or ready to play)?
- ✓ Do I have *multiple* pieces supporting my attack?
